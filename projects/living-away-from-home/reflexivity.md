# Researcher Reflexivity

This memo draws on the submitted SOCY60231 essay and a review of the interview record.

## Revising an initial assumption
Because the interviewer and participant shared some cultural context, the original interview plan assumed previous experience of living independently. This was not the participant's experience. The interview therefore shifted towards their first experience of independent living abroad. Shared cultural background supported rapport but did not guarantee shared biographies.

## Interview interaction
The interviewer sometimes interrupted while the participant was explaining abstract ideas. Subsequent reflection identified a tension between following the topic guide and giving the participant space to develop their own meanings. In a future interview, pauses and open follow-up questions could be used more deliberately.

## Question framing
At one point, the interviewer suggested that the participant's approach might be associated with East Asian cultural expectations. The participant challenged broad generalisations. This is a useful example of why the researcher should test their own explanatory frame instead of expecting agreement.

## Lessons for future projects
- Pilot the guide and examine biographical assumptions.
- Ask open questions before presenting an interpretation.
- Use reflective memos and preserve a dated coding log.
- Treat rapport and researcher positionality as both methodological opportunities and limitations.

These are reflections on the completed coursework, not a claim of a subsequent interview study.
