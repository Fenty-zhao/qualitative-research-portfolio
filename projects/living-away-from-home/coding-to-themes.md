# From Interview Excerpts to Themes

[← Case study](README.md) · [Researcher reflexivity](reflexivity.md) · [Methods & ethics](methods-and-ethics.md)

This page shows examples of how interview material informed the thematic analysis. The **named codes under Theme 1** appear in the surviving Chinese working draft. For portfolio presentation, I linked selected excerpts and paraphrases to those working codes and to the three themes in the submitted report.

## Coding example 01 · What counts as a challenge?

> “I don't see many things in everyday life as challenges. Something comes up, and I just deal with it.”

*Short English translation of the Chinese interview, approximately 05:57.*

**Working codes:** High Challenge Threshold · Normalization of Obstacles · De-dramatization

**Interpretation:** The participant distinguished ordinary problems from situations she considered genuine challenges. When asked what would count, she joked about a television obstacle-course competition and also identified major decisions such as finding work or applying for a PhD.

**Theme 1:** Treating difficulties as “just something to deal with”

## Coding example 02 · What does “leaving home” mean?

The participant explained that “leaving home” was not necessarily an abstract idea: it could simply mean physically going elsewhere to study or work.

*Anonymised paraphrase of the interview, approximately 04:53.*

**Working codes:** Functional Separation · Pragmatic Understanding of Mobility

**Interpretation:** She used a practical description of geographical separation rather than automatically framing the experience as an emotional rupture.

**Theme 1:** Treating difficulties as “just something to deal with”

---

## Coding example 03 · Acting before processing emotions

The participant described wanting to complete an unresolved task before turning to her feelings about it. The final report also discusses an incident involving a valued sweater damaged in the wash: she looked for a practical remedy despite feeling upset.

*Paraphrase of the interview and submitted report. The available transcript excerpt includes discussion at approximately 00:39–01:34.*

**Analytical descriptors:** Task-oriented coping · Emotional deferral

**Interpretation:** Emotion remained present, but action took priority while the situation still required attention.

**Theme 2:** Task-oriented coping and emotional deferral

---

## Coding example 04 · Rejecting a single student narrative

The participant objected to exaggerated portrayals of international-student hardship being treated as representative of everyone studying abroad. She also questioned dismissive stereotypes about international Master's students.

*Anonymised paraphrase from the submitted report.*

**Analytical descriptors:** Narrative resistance · Responses to stereotyping

**Interpretation:** The participant argued for the ability to describe her own experiences rather than be defined by an outside narrative.

**Theme 3:** Responding to social and identity challenges through subjectivity construction

---

## How the themes connect

| Analytical focus | Theme | What it adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meaning** | Treating difficulties as manageable everyday matters | How an experience is defined |
| **Action** | Task-oriented coping and emotional deferral | What the participant does when problems occur |
| **Identity** | Subjectivity construction | How the participant responds to external representations |

These are related interpretations, rather than claims that each theme occurs independently or in a fixed sequence.

## Source and coding notes

The saved materials include a Chinese transcript excerpt, an early Chinese draft listing working codes for the first theme, an English draft, and the submitted report. The links between extracts, codes and themes on this page were **organised retrospectively** for the portfolio. The descriptors for Themes 2 and 3 are drawn from the thematic interpretations; they are **not represented as a recovered original first-cycle codebook**.

The analysis involved manual interpretation and AI-assisted organisation and writing. Full transcripts, names and identifying details are not published. The participant agreed to anonymised use in the public portfolio.
