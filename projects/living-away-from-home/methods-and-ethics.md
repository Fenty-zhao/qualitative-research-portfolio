# Methods, Ethics, and Source Transparency

## Design
This coursework mini-project used a single online, semi-structured interview with a Chinese international Master's student. I contacted a classmate I already knew and thought would be willing to participate; she met the study's basic criteria. The submitted report called this **purposive sampling**, while the actual recruitment route also reflects **convenience-based recruitment**. The report describes a participant information sheet and informed consent.

## Public portfolio consent
The participant has agreed to the use of **anonymised interview excerpts** in a public portfolio. This repository does not include the unredacted transcript, recording, consent form, or complete original drafts.

## Source trail
The portfolio was reconstructed from four existing documents:
1. Chinese interview transcript with timestamps
2. Chinese working draft containing selected named codes
3. English draft
4. Final submitted qualitative methods essay

The [coding table](coding-to-themes.md) links these materials for presentation. It is a **retrospective mapping**, not the original complete line-by-line coding log.

## AI-assisted workflow
The interview and interpretation involved manual work, alongside AI-assisted organisation of the coding and writing. No NVivo or ATLAS.ti software was used. The project does not claim independently verified intercoder reliability or theoretical saturation.

## Privacy safeguards
Only short, anonymised translations or paraphrases are published. Personal names, precise background details, third-party references, and original raw material are not uploaded. The original material is retained outside this public repository.

## Limits of inference
This single interview supports contextual, interpretive findings. It does not establish population prevalence, causal effects, or representativeness.
