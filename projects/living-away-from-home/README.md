# Making Sense of Challenges While Living Away from Home

**Qualitative research case study · SOCY60231 · University of Manchester**

## Research question

**How does an international master's student make sense of and respond to the challenges of living away from home?**

Rather than assuming that moving abroad is necessarily an emotionally overwhelming experience, the study explored how the participant **defined** difficulties and **responded** to them in everyday life.

## At a glance

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Design | Exploratory qualitative study; one participant |
| Participant | International master's student living independently in the UK (anonymised) |
| Recruitment | Purposive sampling |
| Data collection | One-to-one online semi-structured interview |
| Analysis | Inductive thematic analysis, supported by manual coding and AI-assisted organisation/drafting |
| Scope | Detailed account of one person's experiences; **not** generalisable to all students |

## Research process

### 1. Designing for meaning, not measurement

The question required understanding **how an experience was interpreted** and the reasoning behind responses, rather than estimating prevalence. A semi-structured interview allowed the interviewer to use a prepared guide and pursue unanticipated interpretations.

The guide initially progressed from earlier experiences with family or living arrangements to present-day independent living. The researcher's assumption that the participant had previously lived away from home did not hold. The interviewer adjusted the direction of questioning rather than forcing the originally imagined comparison.

### 2. Gathering the account

The interviewer conducted an online one-to-one interview. Rapport and shared cultural references enabled detailed descriptions but could also lead the researcher to presume a shared understanding. The later analysis explicitly considered this tension.

### 3. Moving from extracts to codes to themes

Interview text was manually reviewed, with AI assistance in organising codes and drafting analysis. The surviving Chinese working draft contains named codes such as **High Challenge Threshold**, **Normalization of Obstacles**, **De-dramatization**, and **Functional Separation**.

See [Coding → Themes](coding-to-themes.md) for traceable examples from anonymised excerpts and surviving draft codes. The mappings in that page were **assembled for this portfolio from saved documents**; they are not presented as an untouched original line-by-line coding log.

### 4. Developing findings

The completed submission reported three themes:

**Theme 1 — Treating difficulties as "just something to deal with"**  
The participant repeatedly framed ordinary problems as practical matters to resolve, reserving the word *challenge* for more consequential situations.

**Theme 2 — Task-oriented coping and emotional deferral**  
Actions were prioritised before the processing of feelings. This was interpreted as temporarily setting aside emotional responses, not lacking emotion.

**Theme 3 — Responding to social and identity challenges through subjectivity construction**  
The participant resisted being represented by one stereotyped account of international-student life and asserted the right to define their own experience.

These are **interpretations grounded in one account**, not measurements of how frequently students behave this way.

### 5. Critically reviewing the research process

The interviewer identified methodological issues after reviewing the exchange, including preconceived assumptions, interruptions while the participant was elaborating abstract ideas, and the possible influence of the interviewer's interpretive framing.

The participant's responses sometimes resisted the interviewer's suggested cultural explanations, underscoring why **probing, silence, and openness to disconfirming accounts** matter.

See [Reflexivity](reflexivity.md) for the detailed account.

## Key limitations

- **N = 1**: useful for exploring subjective meaning; not a basis for claims about international students as a population
- A single interview cannot provide **triangulation** through diaries, observations, or additional participants
- Cultural familiarity may support rapport and simultaneously influence questioning and interpretation
- The original coding record was not saved as a complete line-by-line matrix; some presentation tables are post-submission reconstructions
